📚 How to Use This Tool

  1. Add Regions: Click "+ Add Region" to add oil-producing regions
  2. Enter Data: For each region, input:
    • Region name (e.g., "Saudi Arabia", "Texas")
    • Production capacity in million barrels per day (MMbpd)
    • Marginal cost in dollars per barrel ($/bbl)
  3. Update Curve: Click "Update Supply Curve" to visualize the supply curve
  4. Analyze: The graph will show:
    • Supply curve built from lowest to highest marginal cost
    • Each region's contribution as a step in the curve
    • Total supply capacity and cost distribution
  5. Edit Values: Change capacities or costs and click "Update Supply Curve" to see real-time changes

Key Concepts

  • Supply Curve: Shows the relationship between price and quantity supplied. Regions with lower marginal costs produce first.
  • Marginal Cost: The cost to produce one additional barrel of oil. This determines the order in which regions produce.
  • Capacity: The maximum amount a region can produce. This determines the width of each step in the supply curve.
  • Step Function: The supply curve is a step function because each region has a specific capacity and cost, creating discrete jumps.

Example Scenario

The default data includes major oil-producing regions with representative capacities and costs. You can modify these values to:

  • Simulate changes in production costs due to technology or regulations
  • Analyze the impact of capacity expansions or reductions
  • Understand which regions would produce at different price levels
  • Study the competitiveness of different oil-producing regions
